# Investor services and renovation guides audit

Reviewed and revised September 28, 2026. Scope: 13 owned public HTML pages. All existing files were read in full before editing. Existing navigation, analytics, client portal links, site scripts, canonical URLs, and photo assets were retained. No deployment or remote writes were performed.

## Main findings

1. **High — credibility weakened by vague, repeated claims.** Investor copy repeatedly used phrases such as “execution oversight,” “construction path,” “budget discipline,” “strategy,” “repositioning,” and “exit-ready.” It frequently described how the page was trying to prove credibility rather than explaining the work. Replaced with concrete descriptions of inspections, repairs, allowances, access, trades, selections, and project handoff.
2. **High — investor hub blurred contracting with financial and property services.** It advertised sourcing, lending introductions, comps reports, cap-rate analysis, management referrals, and a report package without showing provider names, samples, delivery terms, or supporting evidence. The live draft now focuses on construction services. These broader offerings should only return after the owner confirms them and supplies specific deliverables and any necessary professional relationships. No profit, ARV, refinance, or approval guarantees added.
3. **High — cost pages did not adequately answer their search intent.** Kitchen content made unsupported “mid-five figures” claims, and bathroom/kitchen pages cited unnamed “recent guides.” Removed unsourced cost ranges and vague third-party references. Guides now explicitly explain that project pricing requires a defined scope, then provide tangible estimate comparison questions and missing-cost checks. Actual company project ranges remain an owner-data task.
4. **Medium — guides sounded templated and overstated authority.** Removed “Authority Guide,” “the bigger truth,” “real scope,” “budget magnets,” “honest scope,” and repetitive conclusions. Changed vague timeline guidance into five questions readers can use with a contractor.
5. **Medium — investor hub was too long and lacked an immediate lead action.** Reduced duplicated service descriptions, added prominent project-review and phone CTAs, shortened sections, and retained useful project photography with plain captions.
6. **Medium — legal/permit content needed attributable current support.** Checked current official Philadelphia guidance. Updated permit distinctions, added building and zoning source links, clarified that EZ means no submitted plans for qualifying work rather than no permit, and explained that Philadelphia rules do not automatically apply to suburban municipalities.
7. **Medium — SEO and presentation details.** Wrote unique matching title/description/social/schema copy; article modified dates reflect this substantive edit; added article images in schema, matching investor breadcrumb schema, accessible guide breadcrumbs, and missing guide-index social metadata. Removed unused legacy inline style blocks on the two rebuilt pages so they use shared page components.

## What the owner needs to do

- **Supply pricing evidence:** gather 3–5 recent completed kitchen, bathroom, and whole-home project totals, dates, scope, material level, and exclusions. Obtain permission for publication. Use that evidence for clearly qualified example budgets; do not publish invented market averages.
- **Confirm broader investor services:** decide whether you actually provide sourcing, comps, lending referrals, management introductions, or written investor reports. Name the responsible provider and supply a redacted sample, fee, timing, and what is excluded before these services return to the page.
- **Document investor projects:** choose at least one completed flip, one rental, and one multifamily job. Provide before/during/after photographs from the same property, actual scope, dates, constraints, and approved client feedback. Do not publish financial outcomes unless supported and authorized.
- **Check captions and rights:** confirm every retained photo is Golden Brick’s work, that client/property permissions allow publication, and that the described phase is accurate. Current copy deliberately avoids fabricated dates, clients, or project identities.
- **Confirm operational commitments:** make sure the real estimate/contract process covers allowances, excluded work, change approval, permit responsibilities, site access, and handoff documents. The revised copy asks owners to agree these items in writing instead of implying that every job already includes the same deliverables.
- **Add the human author:** a named, real company owner or construction lead should review the guides and approve their practical advice. Publish their name and relevant actual experience only after confirmation. Current byline remains the company.
