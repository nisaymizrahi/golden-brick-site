Project Scope

When a Homeowner Needs a General Contractor

A renovation often needs several trades to work in a set order. Plumbing and electrical work come before wall finishes; cabinet installation affects countertop measurements. The general contractor organizes that work and its schedule.

Multiple Trades

Kitchen, bathroom, basement, and full-home work usually requires coordination across demolition, carpentry, plumbing, electrical, drywall, flooring, tile, paint, and finish details.

Older Home Conditions

Uneven floors, old plumbing and electrical, patched framing, narrow access, and prior repairs can affect the right construction sequence.

Occupied-Home Work

When people are living in the house during construction, access, staging, protection, and communication matter as much as the physical work.

Planning Around Rowhomes, Permits, and Tight Access

For city projects, we review delivery access, material storage, shared walls, and existing utilities. Photos, drawings, and an inspection report can help identify items to review at the property.

Permit requirements depend on the project scope. Structural work, additions, layout changes, and plumbing relocation may need drawings, approvals, and inspections beyond those needed for cosmetic updates.

General Contractor FAQ

Questions Before You Request an Estimate

These answers are general starting points. Project requirements depend on the property, scope, and existing conditions.

What should I prepare before requesting an estimate?

Share the property address, photos, rooms involved, occupancy status, your main goals, and any timing constraints.

Do I need a permit for a renovation in Philadelphia?

Permit requirements depend on the scope. Cosmetic updates are different from structural work, additions, layout changes, and plumbing or electrical relocation. Share the proposed work so we can identify which requirements need to be checked. See our Philadelphia permit guide for an overview.

Can you work on older Philadelphia rowhomes?

Yes. We plan rowhome renovations around shared walls, access, existing utilities, and the condition of the structure and floors.

Can I live in my home during renovation?

Sometimes. It depends on the rooms involved, utility interruptions, dust, safety, access, and timeline. We discuss those conditions before deciding whether the home can remain occupied.
