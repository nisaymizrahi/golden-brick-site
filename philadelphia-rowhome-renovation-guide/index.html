Philadelphia renovation guide

Philadelphia Rowhome Renovation Guide

By Golden Brick Construction · Updated September 28, 2026

Shared walls, narrow stairs, older utilities, and limited delivery space all affect a Philadelphia rowhome renovation. Review these conditions during the walkthrough so the estimate accounts for access, repairs, and work beyond the room you want to update.

What to send us
  • Photos of each floor and the basement.
  • Known leaks, repairs, or previous alterations.
  • Which walls or room layouts you want to change.
  • Front and rear access and whether the home is occupied.
At the walkthrough

Look beyond the room you want to renovate

A room renovation can require work elsewhere in the house. Check where plumbing, wiring, and ducts run, and include any related openings and repairs in the estimate.

Shared walls and structure

Discuss wall openings, floor movement, visible masonry issues, and structural concerns with the appropriate professional. Do not assume a wall can be removed from photos alone.

Utilities across floors

A drain, water line, duct, or electrical route may serve more than one level. Include access openings and the repair of affected ceilings and walls.

Deliveries and debris

Check door widths, stairs, rear access, material storage, and the route for removing debris. Large items may need a different delivery plan.

Plan the work together

Decide what stays before deciding what to replace

A kitchen renovation can affect adjoining flooring. A bathroom repair may require opening the ceiling below. An addition can affect the existing roof and room layout. Record those connections so the estimate includes the related work.

Moisture and existing damage

Share leaks and repairs you know about. Inspect accessible problem areas before covering them with new finishes.

Living in the home and working near neighbors

Discuss access, dust protection, utility interruptions, and how to keep neighbors informed about work affecting shared areas.

Repairs you cannot see yet

Ask what cannot be checked until walls or floors are opened, and how any additional repairs will be priced and approved.

Before work starts

Confirm plans and permit requirements

Some structural work and changes to shared walls require additional documentation. Historic status may also affect proposed work. Check the property and scope using the Philadelphia permit guide, which links to official City sources.

Questions before you start

Questions about rowhome renovations

Can I remove a wall to open the first floor?

The wall and the structure above it need to be assessed first. Ask what design, engineering, support, and permit work the proposed opening requires before including it in the budget.

What should I ask about a basement renovation?

Start with moisture, ceiling height, utilities, access, and how you want to use the space. Review those conditions before choosing finishes.

Where should I start with the budget?

List the rooms and systems involved, then separate essential repairs from optional upgrades. Our full-home renovation budget guide explains material allowances, excluded work, and budgeting for repairs that still need investigation.
