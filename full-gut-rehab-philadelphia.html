Define the work

What does a full-gut renovation include?

The term can mean different things in different estimates. Specify which walls, finishes, and systems will be removed, what stays, and what will be rebuilt.

Demolition and repairs

Define the demolition, debris removal, site protection, and known repairs. Identify areas that need further investigation before repair costs can be confirmed.

Framing and systems

Coordinate the approved layout with structural work, plumbing, electrical, and HVAC. Confirm drawings, permit requirements, and specialist responsibilities before starting.

Interior finishes

Specify insulation, drywall, flooring, trim, kitchens, bathrooms, fixtures, and painting. Plan material orders and deliveries around the construction schedule.

Questions before you start

Common Questions About Full-Gut Renovations

How is a gut renovation different from a full-home remodel?

A gut renovation generally removes substantial interior finishes and may replace major systems. A full-home remodel can retain more of the existing walls and systems. The written scope matters more than the label.

Can I stay in the house during the work?

A full-gut renovation can interrupt essential rooms and utilities and may require the property to be vacant. We review the actual scope and occupancy needs before discussing the work schedule.

How much will it cost?

A walkthrough, defined scope, and material selections are needed for a useful estimate. Size alone does not account for structural repairs, systems, access, and the condition behind finished surfaces. See our full-home budget guide.

What happens at the end of the project?

The final walkthrough covers the completed work and any remaining items. The contract should also specify cleanup, project documents, and required inspections.
